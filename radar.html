Radar

Sign in to save Edit ⋮
The Radar is the most critical tool for any storm chaser in Tegree. It is a simulated Doppler radar system that allows you to track precipitation, analyze storm structures, locate your deployed probes, and safely navigate around tornadoes. Mastering the radar is the difference between a successful intercept and being caught off guard.

Quick Controls & Info

  • Toggle Radar: Press the Radar icon on the left sidebar, or press the [M] key on your keyboard.
  • Update Delay: The radar data refreshes every 8 seconds (simulating real-world radar volume scans).
  • Player Tracking: Your position is updated in real-time, while the weather data lags slightly behind.
Radar Modes (Base Products)
The radar system features two primary modes, mimicking real-life NEXRAD (WSR-88D) systems. You can toggle between them using the buttons on the side of the radar screen.
Reflectivity (Base Reflectivity)
Reflectivity measures the amount of microwave energy scattered back to the radar by precipitation (rain, hail, and snow). It is primarily used to determine the intensity and location of precipitation.

In Tegree, higher reflectivity values (usually shown as heavier colors like deep red or purple on the color bar) indicate intense rain or large hail. This mode is best for identifying the hook echo, the FFD (Forward Flank Downdraft), and the inflow notch. However, it does not directly show you the wind speed or rotation.
Velocity (Base Velocity)
Velocity mode uses the Doppler effect to measure the radial velocity—the speed at which precipitation is moving toward or away from the radar dish.

Color Interpretation:
  • Green: Winds moving towards the radar.
  • Red: Winds moving away from the radar.
To identify a tornado or a strong mesocyclone, look for Velocity Couplets: areas where bright green (inbound) and bright red (outbound) are right next to each other. Remember the rule: if you are standing at the radar looking at the storm, rotation exists if the red is on the right and green is on the left (counter-clockwise rotation).

Advanced Tip: Watch out for "Velocity Folding" (where the colors suddenly wrap around to the opposite side of the color scale). This happens when winds exceed the radar's maximum measurable velocity, indicating extremely violent tornado winds.
Map Icons & Data Interpretation
Your Location & Other Players
You are represented on the radar by a yellow/orange arrow. The direction the arrow points is the direction your vehicle is facing. Unlike the weather data, your GPS location updates in real-time.

Other players in the server appear as white arrows with their usernames hovering above them. This is incredibly useful for coordinating intercepts, seeing where your friends are, or knowing if another player is dangerously close to a tornado's path.
Probe Locations (Mesonet Deployments)
Once you deploy a probe (like a Mesonet or a Tornado Pod), a specific probe icon will appear on the radar at its exact location. This is critical for retrieving your data. It allows you to verify if your probe survived the tornado and helps you calculate your path to drive back and collect the wind speed data.
Precipitation Levels & Visibility
At the bottom of the Reflectivity radar screen, there is a color bar indicating precipitation levels.

Gameplay Impact: The higher the precipitation level (the heavier the rain/hail), the harder it will be to see the tornado with your own eyes. If the radar shows deep reds or purples around your position, you are inside the FFD (Forward Flank Downdraft), and your in-game visibility will be drastically reduced. Use the radar to guide you out of the rain and into the clear inflow notch for a better view.
Tactical Radar Usage
Don't just stare at the screen. Use the 8-second delay to your advantage:
  • Trend Analysis: A single frame of radar doesn't tell you much. Watch the hook echo over multiple 8-second updates. Is the hook tightening? Is the velocity couplet getting stronger? This tells you if the tornado is intensifying.
  • Storm Motion: By watching the radar blobs move, calculate the storm's speed and direction. This helps you decide where to position your vehicle before the tornado arrives, rather than reacting too late.
  • RFD Danger: Keep an eye on the velocity mode. Sometimes a strong RFD (Rear Flank Downdraft) can look like a tornado on velocity. If you don't see a tight, clear velocity couplet, it might just be straight-line winds from the RFD.
